What Is a Notice Period?

A notice period is the period of time an employee is expected to continue working after formally submitting a resignation, subject to the terms of the employment agreement and applicable rules. It gives the employer time to plan the employee's replacement, transfer responsibilities, complete documentation, and manage the transition.

For employees, the notice period provides time to complete current assignments, document important information, hand over responsibilities, and prepare for the next stage of their career. The length of a notice period can differ depending on the employment contract, job role, employer policy, location, and applicable regulations.

Common Notice Period Durations

Notice periods can vary considerably between organizations and employment arrangements. Common examples include 15 days, 30 days, 45 days, 60 days, and 90 days. Some senior, specialized, or contractual positions may have longer notice requirements.

These durations are examples rather than universal requirements. The notice period that applies to you should be confirmed from your employment agreement, HR policy, appointment letter, or other applicable employment documentation.

How to Calculate Your Notice Period Manually

A basic notice-period calculation involves adding the applicable notice duration to the relevant resignation date. For example, if your employment agreement specifies a 30-day notice period, you can count the applicable calendar days from the resignation date according to the method specified by your employer.

The exact result can depend on how the employment agreement defines the notice period. Some organizations refer to a specific number of calendar days, while others may use working days or define the period in terms of months. This is why the calculator result should be treated as an estimate unless it matches the calculation method specified in your employment documentation.

30-Day Notice Period Example

Whether the resignation date itself is counted as day one can depend on the wording of the employment agreement or company policy. For an important employment decision, confirm the counting method with HR.

Calendar Days vs Working Days

One of the most common sources of confusion is the difference between calendar days and working days.

Calendar Days

Calendar days generally include every day on the calendar, including weekends and holidays. If a contract specifies a notice period in calendar days, Saturdays, Sundays, and applicable holidays may be included in the period.

Working Days

A working-day calculation may exclude weekends and certain holidays. If your employment agreement specifically defines the notice period using working days, the final date can be different from a calculation based on calendar days.

Always check the wording used in your employment agreement before deciding which calculation method applies.

Does a Notice Period Include Weekends?

It depends on how the notice period is defined. When a contract specifies a period in calendar days, weekends are generally part of the calendar. When the agreement specifically uses working or business days, weekends may be excluded.

Company holidays and other non-working days can also affect calculations when the applicable policy uses working days. If there is any uncertainty, HR should confirm how your organization calculates the notice period.

Does a Public Holiday Count During the Notice Period?

A public holiday may count toward a notice period when the notice is defined in calendar days. However, the treatment can be different when the agreement refers to working days or contains specific provisions regarding holidays.

The Notice Period Calculator provides a date estimate based on the inputs and calculation method supported by the tool. It does not replace your employer's official calculation or HR confirmation.

Can You Leave Before Completing Your Notice Period?

An employee may be able to leave before completing the full notice period if the employer agrees to an early release or another arrangement permitted by the employment agreement and company policy.

Possible arrangements can include an approved early release, notice-period adjustment, or notice buyout. The availability and terms of these options vary between employers and contracts.

What Is Notice Period Buyout?

A notice-period buyout generally refers to an arrangement where part or all of the remaining notice period is compensated financially instead of being served through continued work. Whether a buyout is available, who can request it, and how the amount is calculated depends on the employment agreement and employer policy.

Employees considering a buyout should obtain confirmation from HR about the applicable amount, approval process, final settlement, and expected release date. Do not assume that a buyout is automatically available in every employment situation.

Can You Take Leave During Your Notice Period?

Whether an employee can use accumulated leave during a notice period depends on company policy, employment terms, and approval from the employer. Some organizations may allow certain types of leave, while others may restrict leave during the exit process.

Approved leave does not necessarily reduce the contractual notice period. In some cases, leave may affect the actual working days while the official employment end date remains unchanged. Always confirm the treatment of leave with HR.

What If Your Employer Releases You Early?

An employer may agree to release an employee before the originally estimated notice end date. The actual release date should be confirmed formally by HR or the appropriate employer representative.

If an early-release request is approved, keep written confirmation of the agreed last working day and any related terms. This can help avoid confusion during final settlement and when communicating the joining date to a new employer.

Notice Period in India

In India, notice periods commonly appear in employment contracts and company policies, particularly for corporate and professional roles. Depending on the employer and role, employees may encounter notice periods such as 30, 60, or 90 days, although other durations are also possible.

There is no single notice-period duration that applies to every employee in every situation. The applicable requirement can depend on the employment contract, company policy, employment arrangement, location, and relevant laws or regulations.

If your employment situation involves a dispute, contractual penalty, resignation restriction, salary recovery, or other legal issue, consider obtaining appropriate professional advice rather than relying solely on an online calculator.

Notice Period and Your New Job Joining Date

Calculating your estimated last working day can help when discussing a joining date with a prospective employer. Before committing to a joining date, consider the possibility of changes caused by early release negotiations, leave approval, notice buyout arrangements, or differences in how the current employer counts the notice period.

It is generally useful to obtain confirmation of your expected release date before making firm commitments that depend on your employment end date.

Best Practices During Your Notice Period

  • Review your employment agreement: Confirm the stated notice duration and how the period is calculated.
  • Submit your resignation through the required channel: Follow your organization's official resignation process.
  • Confirm your expected last working day: Ask HR or your manager to confirm the date when necessary.
  • Complete your knowledge transfer: Document project information, processes, responsibilities, and important operational details.
  • Complete pending responsibilities: Prioritize important tasks and communicate their status clearly.
  • Return company property: Complete the required process for laptops, access cards, equipment, and other company assets.
  • Keep important exit documents: Follow your employer's process for obtaining applicable employment and exit documentation.

Knowledge Transfer During the Notice Period

A well-organized knowledge transfer can make the transition easier for both the employee and the organization. Depending on your role, useful handover information may include project status, documentation, recurring tasks, known issues, important contacts, process notes, and outstanding work.

Avoid sharing confidential company information outside authorized systems. Follow your employer's information-security, data-protection, and document-retention policies throughout the notice period.
